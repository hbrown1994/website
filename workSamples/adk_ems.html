Derivatives — stereo and multichannel fixed-media composition, 2024



External Link: https://samply.app/p/gBTEn9tWS3f7tFVGZkN2?si=LjdLyzc30ccN5T42lKEf4tI0XEw1

Derivatives is a work that explores the disjunctions between human and machine auditory perception. It juxtaposes real-world sound objects with their computational abstractions and synthetic digital representations, guiding the listener’s ear toward the boundary between the real and the simulated. This is explored through field recordings taken in Buenos Aires and Chicago and AI-synthesized speech. Through source separation, psychoacoustically informed spectral analysis, onset detection, and spectral resynthesis, the source materials' spectral components, transients, residual spectra, and rhythmic structures are extracted and reorganized into new compositional forms.

The composition was created for a multimedia dance work that premiered at the 2024 Festival Nueva Ópera in Buenos Aires.

Latent Enclosures — multichannel sound installation with custom acoustic-analysis system, 2025



External Link: https://youtu.be/ZucBNAoUZwE

Latent Enclosures is a site-adaptable sound installation developed and presented at the 2025 Ars Electronica Festival that explores how machine listening systems perceive, transform, and rearticulate architectural acoustics.

Using impulse response analysis and real-time resynthesis, the work reveals latent sonic properties of enclosed spaces and allows listeners’ movements within the installation to dynamically alter its acoustic behavior.

Stoppages Vol. 2 — stereo fixed-media composition, 2024




External Link: https://alwayspartyperfect.bandcamp.com/album/pp-10-stoppages-vol-2

Stoppages Vol. 2 investigates the material nature of digital audio through manipulations of the MP3 codec. The MP3 employs perceptual audio coding, an encoding process that intentionally discards information deemed “perceptually irrelevant” by psychoacoustic models of human hearing in order to reduce file size and facilitate reproduction and distribution. Embedded within this process is an assumed universal mode of listening in which timbral delicacy, Western tonality, and naturality are paramount; Suzanne Vega’s “Tom’s Diner,” for example, served as one of the sonic benchmarks used to tune the format. This collection of twenty compositions intervenes in and subverts the MP3’s perceptual coding process by processing sounds whose qualities oppose those the codec is designed to preserve and by directly altering its psychoacoustic models. Using the distinctly digital material of Stoppages Vol. 1 as its source, these manipulations transform the MP3 into a generative process that exposes the latent properties of this standardized format and reorients the ear beyond its supposed universal mode of listening toward an alternative listening, where something other can emerge.
